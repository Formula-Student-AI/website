It was the first morning of Formula Student 2026. The sun was out, the roar of the cars on the track was deafening, and the air smelt of asphalt. And before FSAI registration was even open, we'd arrived - we were back, and we were ready for our second year. 

## Travel

Over 2026, FSAI's autonomous driving improved a lot. Unfortunately this was not true for human driving, where we went from two drivers to one, despite three more people coming to competition.

We even managed a trip to Sainsbury's to buy pizza/pasta/cereal/Monsters to keep us going, but there was no time to be distracted by the breathtaking majesty of Milton Keynes - the first day of Formula Student was soon upon us. 


### Arriving at Silverstone

The atmosphere at Silverstone was very different to anywhere else I'd been. From the moment you walk through the gates, you're deafened by cars screaming around the track, and the air smells of oil and rubber.

## Dynamic Driving Task (DDT): Scrutineering

Our first objective was to pass DDT scrutineering. This involves showing that you can get the car's wheels to turn and have it execute simple maneuvers without triggering the very easy-to-trigger emergency brake. Even though we'd already tested on the car, getting our system to run was difficult, and we had plenty of bugs to fix. 

The coding tent was where we did most of our work over the first couple of days.

Every team had a table, and teams were very different - some were big teams with shirts covered in sponsorship logos, some were just a few friends, some brought in 9-packs of Red Bull every day, and there was one team who would wheel around a large PC mounted in a shopping trolley so that they could use it for training. 

We were happy to get a spot next to Bath FSAI. They were great allies to us throughout the year - they had many more years of experience than us, but were nevertheless always happy to give us advice and show us how to do things.

## DDT: Dynamic Events

After passing scrutineering on the simulator, and then on the real car, we were able to enter dynamic events. In between, we made use of the valuable opportunities to test on the ADS-DVs. 

<figure style="margin: 2rem 0;">
        <video autoplay loop muted playsinline webkit-playsinline aria-label="A testing run into a cone" style="display: block; width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 10px; object-fit: contain; margin: 0;" src="/posts/silverstone_2026/our_system_drives_straight_into_cone.MP4">Your browser does not support embedded video. <a href="/posts/silverstone_2026/our_system_drives_straight_into_cone.MP4">Watch the video</a>.</video>

In the end, we earned 30 points for our four laps of the track, putting us in 7th for trackdrive. We estimate we could have earned just under 100, as the system was navigating the track successfully every time, but we were prematurely cut off as the time allocated for the event was nearly up and other teams wanted one last attempt before then. If we'd been earlier in the queue, we probably would have placed in the top 5!  

Also, we learned so much during the week that our system on Sunday would have earned lots of points in the earlier events, but the window for them was over. Next year, though, we'll be starting from a system that can complete every event (albeit slowly), so we are excited to see how far we can take it.

## Autonomous Platform Cup (APC)

Our APC team had worked hard to design an autonomous system built on a chassis UWE had agreed to give us for 2027's competition. Mithun and the subteam leads presented slides and a CAD model in the Autonomous System Design presentation, and received both very positive feedback and lots of useful advice for next year.

We were expecting a battle with two other concept entries for third place, but clearly they'd heard that we were in the running as they both conceded gracefully before the week started. The first of many podium finishes!

## DDT: Static Events

The static events were a really good opportunity to talk properly to the judges and to get feedback.
